# Checkpoint 5 mandatory readiness criteria

Apply every gate. N/A requires a project-specific reason or evidenced exception.

## Mandatory gates

### C1 Fixed final package and route

Identify the final thesis PDF and exact source revision. The package includes the complete source, dependency/environment manifests, setup/run guidance or README, required artifacts, and signed AI declaration as a separate file when current policy requires it. State the normal Neptun or verified deferred route.

### C2 Compulsory structure and front matter

Include a cover whose registered data match Neptun, the approved thesis proposal form issued after approval, table of contents, user documentation, developer documentation, bibliography, and applicable lists/appendices. An abstract and acknowledgements remain optional unless current rules say otherwise.

### C3 Approved promise completed

The delivered system and thesis satisfy the approved topic, exactly three contributions, requirements, and accepted scope changes. Each contribution has evidence through design, implementation, testing, user documentation, and demonstration. An unapproved missing promised contribution fails even if disclosed as future work.

### C4 User documentation

Provide at least twenty substantive pages addressing:

- purpose, target audience, usage context, prerequisites, and minimum/optimal hardware/software;
- installation/setup/start steps verified against a clean or equivalent environment;
- user workflows and all promised complex features, supported by legible screenshots where appropriate;
- roles/user levels, runtime messages, operational/security guidance, troubleshooting, limitations, and FAQ as applicable.

### C5 Design documentation

Provide at least ten substantive pages and three to four high-quality figures. Cover requirements, architecture, mandatory class diagram, business/core logic, data design, and UI/CLI/API interaction design as applicable. Major decisions have rationale and match the final system.

### C6 Implementation documentation

Provide at least ten substantive pages covering environment/stack, repository/dependency structure, implementation decisions, realized complex features, important code snippets, design correspondence/deviations, error/resource handling, maintainability, and required shortcomings/known bugs/technical debt/future work.

### C7 Testing documentation

Provide at least ten substantive pages. Include strategy, unit/functional, UI where applicable, integration/end-to-end, regression, and relevant advanced reliability testing such as performance, stress, fuzz, security, communication, or authentication. Test cases state steps/inputs, expected result, and observed result. Include datasets, edge/failure cases, scalability/high-volume behavior, correctness analysis, efficiency, and lessons that changed design/implementation. AI systems require quantitative metrics, baselines, and error analysis.

### C8 Conclusion and bibliography

The conclusion uses two to four substantive pages to compare achieved goals with the proposal, reflect on challenges/lessons, and state limitations/future work. The bibliography has at least thirty actually used, correctly cited items; fifty is recommended. Standards, RFCs, tools, platforms, libraries, books, and papers may count when genuinely used.

### C9 Final-exam topics

Exactly three registered topics are authentic matches. Every registered subtopic has substantive thesis-specific coverage in the appropriate design, implementation, or testing location and corresponding technical evidence. Coverage emerges naturally; generic textbook summaries and headings such as Exam Topic 1 do not pass.

### C10 Program execution

Demonstrated software:

- behaves according to the task definition and solution plan;
- completes all promised workflows;
- handles realistic user errors and data volumes;
- does not crash under ordinary ethical testing;
- provides clear, usable interactions, messages, labels, help, and safe interruption where relevant.

For headless/library systems, translate UI-only checks into API/CLI usability and operational safety rather than marking execution N/A.

### C11 Source, thesis, and demo identity

The demonstrated software comes from the submitted revision. Documented installation and behavior match actual execution. Diagrams, screenshots, APIs, schema, classes, tests, version numbers, limitations, and source do not contradict one another.

### C12 Reproducibility and deployment

A new evaluator can install/configure/run a representative path from the supplied instructions and artifacts in a safe environment. External dependencies, credentials placeholders, datasets, services, versions, licenses, and fallback demo arrangements are explicit.

### C13 AI compliance

Apply the current faculty policy reflected by the supervisor site. The signed separate declaration accurately lists every AI tool, purpose, method, and location; point-of-use footnotes/code comments agree with it; generated material has been technically verified and critically reviewed. Do not guess whether AI was used.

### C14 Scholarly and visual integrity

The thesis is logically structured, clear, edited, and visually readable. Numbering, headings, contents, captions, cross-references, figures, tables, code excerpts, and bibliography are consistent. Quotation, paraphrase, images, code, datasets, and external material are properly attributed and license-compatible.

### C15 Submission-process readiness

Required names, archive structure, upload limits, and semester instructions are known and satisfied. The student has planned the single allowed Turnitin pre-check under the supervisor's snapshot and understands that repeated uploads can delay reports. Do not fail solely because a pre-check is not yet run unless current instructions make it mandatory before this checkpoint.

## Guidance, not automatic gates

- Typical strong total thesis length is 60-70 pages; 70-80 may support a very high grade when quality remains strong. Do not fail solely on total pages when every minimum and substantive requirement passes.
- A mock defense one to two weeks before the final examination is recommended.
- Similarity percentages around ordinary technical/template/reference overlap require interpretation and are not proof of misconduct.
