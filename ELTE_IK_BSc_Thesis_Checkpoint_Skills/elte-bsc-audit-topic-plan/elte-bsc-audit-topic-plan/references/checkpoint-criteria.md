# Checkpoint 1 mandatory criteria

Apply every gate. N/A is permitted only where stated.

## Expected evidence

- Completed registration-form fields or the form itself.
- A concise proposal of approximately 300-500 words, fitting roughly one-half to one page.
- A dated work plan covering the thesis lifecycle.
- The next meeting date and the concrete progress due then.
- Any approved exception or scope change.

## Mandatory gates

### C1 Registered problem and deliverable

The title, problem, goal, and software/system to be delivered are concrete and mutually consistent. The proposal uses delivery language rather than open-ended verbs such as merely explore or investigate. It remains flexible about design choices that have not been approved.

Fail if a reasonable evaluator could not later decide whether the promised system was delivered.

### C2 Exactly three headline contributions

The compact proposal presents exactly three major technical contributions. Each is non-trivial, technically meaningful, and independently assessable; a contribution may bundle related subfeatures. This reconciles the homepage's minimum of three complex features with the dedicated proposal prompt's exactly-three format.

Fail for fewer or more headline items, three cosmetic/UI-only variations, or artificial splitting of one feature.

### C3 Acceptance signals

For each contribution, state:

- intended user-visible or system behavior;
- a measurable completion signal;
- the evidence expected at evaluation, such as a runnable workflow, output, benchmark, test, or integration demonstration.

The signal must distinguish done from partially done.

### C4 Difficulty and feasible scope

The work is credibly thesis-level and feasible in the schedule. Consider non-obvious algorithms, integration across components/platforms/services, data/persistence complexity, larger-system context, or knowledge beyond compulsory coursework. Do not require every example.

For a UI-centered project, the proposal explicitly targets about 2,000-3,000 lines of meaningful student-authored code, excluding generated code, boilerplate, and dependencies. For a non-UI or atypical project, this line-count condition may be N/A only if the proposal gives an equivalent complexity rationale.

### C5 Deliverables and prerequisites

The proposal briefly commits to:

- an executable system or prototype;
- source code;
- technical documentation;
- demonstration material;
- required knowledge/tools;
- classification as engineering, research, or mixed.

If a live supervisor page has changed these fields, use the live values and report the difference. Missing or altered mandatory text is a repairable failure.

### C7 Proposal form and readability

The proposal follows the dedicated compact structure: title; Problem and Goal; Proposed System; three Core Contributions; Deliverables and Requirements. It is dense, formal, project-specific, and readable. Layout defects that obscure content fail this gate; minor style improvements do not.

### C8 Work plan

The plan maps concrete outputs to all five official checkpoint dates, accounts for implementation, testing, documentation, submission, and review time, and identifies dependencies or high-risk work early. It must not end-load nearly all implementation.

### C9 Next consultation

The next meeting date or scheduling commitment and the exact progress due before it are recorded.

### C10 Cross-document consistency

Title, scope, contributions, deliverables, technologies, and dates do not conflict between the form, proposal, and plan. Any deliberate difference is explained as an approved revision.

## Non-blocking suggestions

When the gates pass, comments may still recommend clearer risk buffers, better acceptance evidence, early repository setup, citation tracking, or planning for AI-use disclosure. Label these as suggestions; do not use them to reverse a sufficient result.
