The strategic point
Almost every classical dynamic graph problem now sits in one of three states: closed at the top (decremental SCC is essentially settled at near-linear total time), blocked by OMv/SETH conditional lower bounds (fully dynamic reachability, transitive closure, s-t distances), or already has a hard-to-beat bound. Trying to shave those is how a student burns a year.
The reliable route to a genuine asymptotic result is to pick a structure for which no dynamic algorithm exists at all, so that anything beating recompute-from-scratch is a first. Those still exist, and they cluster around compiler-flavoured and graph-search-flavoured structures rather than connectivity/distance ones.
Also relevant to positioning: Georgiadis–Italiano–Parotsidis already have decremental dominators in O(mn log n) total, plus decremental strong bridges built on an in-out dominator tree per SCC (arXiv 1704.08235). So decremental strong bridges/SAPs are not free corollary territory anymore, and the dominators paper with Kozsik needs to be explicit about what the O(mn) claim buys over that. uoi
Tier A: adjacent to machinery you already have
Irreducible loop nesting forests, decrementally, with a bound. There's an April 2026 arXiv preprint (2604.13664) that does the first fully dynamic LNF maintenance for reducible control-flow graphs, using dynamic DFS to confine updates to local DFST regions — but its guarantees are locality propositions and correctness theorems, not a total-time bound. Two open holes: the irreducible case (Ramalingam/Havlak/Steensgaard forests diverge there), and an actual amortized total-time bound over a deletion sequence. Since Ramalingam's LNF is recursive SCC contraction on DFS-restricted subgraphs, and decremental SCC is now near-linear, there's a plausible Õ(m·polylog) or O(mn) decremental LNF waiting to be written. This is the single best target on the list. arxivarxiv
Edge-dynamic minimal triangulation. LEX M and MCS-M produce minimal triangulations of arbitrary graphs in O(nm) statically. The dynamic literature covers chordal maintenance well — Ibarra's approach plus improved O(n + k²) deletion updates and a Ω(log n) cell-probe lower bound — but for minimal triangulation of a non-chordal graph, the only online result is Berry et al.'s vertex-incremental O(nm) algorithm, where vertices arrive in any order. Edge-dynamic minimal triangulation looks genuinely untouched, and LEX M is LexBFS with fill paths, so your pivot-tree certificate is the natural substrate. Strong fit, clearly journal-sized. springersciencedirect
Parameterize by loop connectedness. For dominators/LNF on real CFGs, d(G) (max back edges on a DFS-acyclic path) is tiny — Knuth measured ≤3 almost always. An O(md) or O(m + nd²) decremental bound instead of O(mn) is a real asymptotic contribution, is provable rather than hoped-for, and explains the practical behaviour referees always ask about. Undersold area.
Tier B: formulaic but genuinely publishable
Dynamic MCS / LexDFS / MNS certificates. Direct extension of the pivot-tree paper. MCS has the downstream payoff (chordality, minimal triangulation, treewidth heuristics); LexDFS is harder and more interesting since it underpins cocomparability-graph algorithms.
Dynamic multi-sweep search. LBFS+, LBFS*, the 4-sweep diameter estimator. Maintaining sweep certificates under updates is novel and nobody has done it.
Sensitivity oracles instead of full dynamism. Preprocess once, answer "what is the structure after deleting these d edges" in sublinear time. Far easier than fully dynamic, still counts as a clean asymptotic result, and the fault-tolerant literature has never touched loop nesting forests or graph-search orders.
Fully dynamic recognition of a class that lacks one. The Crespelle–Paul template (cographs, permutation, interval, distance-hereditary all done at O(n)/update). Trapezoid graphs and some circular-arc subclasses look unclaimed. Reliable acceptance, modest glory.
Avoid
Incremental DFS trees in general digraphs (long-standing open, not low-hanging), anything fully dynamic on reachability or distances, decremental SCC improvements, dynamic treewidth or planarity.
For the agentic workflow angle
The problems above share a property that makes them unusually good targets for automated exploration: every one has a cheap independent certificate — a PEO, a dominator tree verifiable in linear time, an LNF checkable by header/body invariants, a triangulation checkable for chordality and minimality. That means an agent loop can propose a repair rule, exhaustively test it on all small graphs, and get a hard refutation signal rather than a vibe. Problems without cheap certificates (distance oracles, matchings) waste agent cycles. I'd have the student filter candidates on certificate cost first.
