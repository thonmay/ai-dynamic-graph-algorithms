Title: How rigid is maximum cardinality search? Stability and locality of MCS orderings under edge updates, verified in Lean
Authors: Md Thoriqul Islam Thonmay, Gregory Morse
Abstract: For which sets F of edge modifications do a graph G and the modified graph share a maximum cardinality search (MCS) ordering? We prove that they do whenever, along every proper common MCS prefix, each unchosen vertex's neighbour count in the second graph exceeds its count in the first by zero or one. In particular, inserting or deleting any matching, or flipping a single edge, preserves a common ordering, and under the same hypothesis every common MCS prefix extends to a full common ordering without backtracking. Neither part of the hypothesis can simply be dropped: inserting a triangle, a P_4 or a 2P_3 shows that the +1 bound cannot be relaxed to +2, and a disjoint mixed insert/delete pair shows that the one-directional condition is needed. For the canonical ordering (smallest-index tie-breaking) under a single-edge update, an insertion can first change the ordering only strictly between the endpoints' positions, and a deletion only at the later endpoint; when no break fires, the ordering is unchanged. A boolean-adjacency executable refines the abstract probe-then-repair update, and a no-break deletion costs exactly one legality check. All of these results, including the four obstructions, are proved in Lean 4 over Mathlib and re-checked by an external kernel checker. On paper we further show that the first break of an insertion is located in O((deg(v)+1) log n) time, independent of the window length. We claim no worst-case asymptotic speedup over recomputation.
