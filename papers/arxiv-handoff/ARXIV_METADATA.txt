Title: Locality and stability of maximum cardinality search orderings under edge updates, with a machine-checked implementation
Authors: Md Thoriqul Islam Thonmay, Gregory Morse
Abstract: We maintain the canonical maximum cardinality search (MCS) vertex ordering of an undirected graph under a single-edge update (insertion or deletion), rather than recompute it: every update either leaves the ordering unchanged or re-greedily rebuilds one suffix, under smallest-index tie-breaking. We give a machine-checked proof, in Lean 4 over Mathlib, of the update correctness, of the exact locality of a single-edge update (the first change lies in an open window between the endpoints; for a deletion, at one position), of the refinement of a boolean-adjacency executable to the abstract update, and of a probe/pick count. The central structural result is a stability theorem together with certified obstructions: the set-valued MCS orderings of two graphs intersect whenever the two graphs track one another at distance at most one along every common MCS prefix; inserting or deleting a matching, or flipping a single edge, always preserves a common MCS ordering, while no common ordering survives inserting a triangle, a P_4, a 2P_3, or a disjoint mixed pair. All positive results and the four certified obstructions are proved in Lean and kernel-rechecked. We also prove a sublinear first-break probe: the insertion probe is answerable in O((deg(v)+1) log n) time, independent of the window length, so a no-change update can be certified in o(n) for sparse endpoints. To our knowledge this is the first locality analysis and first machine-checked incremental repair of an MCS ordering under edge updates.
