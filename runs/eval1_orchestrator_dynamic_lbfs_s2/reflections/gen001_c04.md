## FAILURE_DESCRIPTION
Operation: `insert (2, 0)` at sequence_idx 0 (i.e., right after `init`).
The candidate returned ordering `[0, 2, 1, 3]`, but the verifier reports it is NOT a valid LexBFS ordering of the post-insertion graph. The violation is at positions (a,b,c) = (1,2,3), i.e. vertices (va, vb, vc) = (2, 1, 3):
- va=2 and vb=1 are NOT adjacent (ab not in E),
- va=2 and vc=3 ARE adjacent (ac in E),
- no d < a (only d=0, vertex 0) satisfies db in E and dc not in E (violation field `minD: 1000000000` = sentinel for "no such d found").

Expected (one valid) ordering: `[0, 2, 3, 1]`.

## ROOT_CAUSE
The static `_lexbfs` is correct, but the post-insertion validity check `_is_valid_lexbfs` is WRONG, so `insert_edge` keeps an invalid ordering instead of recomputing.

The (LB) 4-point property (Corneil–Krueger) is: for all positions a < b < c with **ab in E and ac not in E**, there exists d < a with **db not in E and dc in E**.

The candidate's `_is_valid_lexbfs` implements the EXACTLY INVERTED condition:
```python
if adj[va][vb]:
    continue  # ab in E, skip          <- should be: skip when ab NOT in E
...
if not adj[va][vc]:
    continue  # ac not in E, skip      <- should be: skip when ac IN E
...
if adj[vd][vb] and not adj[vd][vc]:    <- should be: not adj[vd][vb] and adj[vd][vc]
```
So it checks "for all a<b<c with ac in E and ab not in E, exists d<a with db in E and dc not in E" — the negation of the real property.

Consequences, traced on the failing case:
1. The checker accepts `[0, 2, 1, 3]` as valid (returns True) even though it violates the true (LB) property at (a,b,c)=(1,2,3) with vertices (2,1,3). So `insert_edge` returns the stale state unchanged.
2. Symmetrically, the checker would REJECT genuinely valid orderings (e.g. `[0, 2, 3, 1]`), forcing unnecessary recomputes — but the correctness bug is the false-accept in (1).

Note the docstring of `_is_valid_lexbfs` also states the inverted property, so the inversion is systematic, not a one-off typo.

## REPAIR_HINTS
Fix `_is_valid_lexbfs` to implement the true (LB) property. Minimal change, three inversions:
1. `if adj[va][vb]: continue`  →  `if not adj[va][vb]: continue` (only consider pairs with ab in E).
2. `if not adj[va][vc]: continue`  →  `if adj[va][vc]: continue` (only consider c with ac not in E).
3. `if adj[vd][vb] and not adj[vd][vc]:`  →  `if not adj[vd][vb] and adj[vd][vc]:` (witness d must be adjacent to c and NOT to b).

After this fix, the failing case works: `_is_valid_lexbfs` correctly rejects `[0, 2, 1, 3]` (no witness d for (2,1,3)), `insert_edge` falls through to `_lexbfs`, and the recomputed ordering is valid.

Do NOT try to "repair" the ordering in place or change the recompute fallback — the static `_lexbfs` and the recompute-on-invalid strategy are fine; the only defect is the inverted certificate. Also do not change the property to a different characterization; the (LB) 4-point property as stated in the brief (ab in E, ac not in E, witness d with db not in E and dc in E) is the correct one to implement.
