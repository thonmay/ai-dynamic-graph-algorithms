## FAILURE_DESCRIPTION
The runner crashed with "Invalid output" because the candidate printed to stdout. The printed text is a series of `MISMATCH: _check_lb=False, _check_lb_brute=True` lines, all for the same 3-vertex graph:

- order = [1, 0, 2]
- adj[1] = 0b1  (vertex 1 adjacent to vertex 0)
- adj[0] = 0b110 (vertex 0 adjacent to vertices 1 and 2)
- adj[2] = 0b1  (vertex 2 adjacent to vertex 0)

The graph is the path 1—0—2. The ordering [1, 0, 2] is a valid LexBFS ordering (start at 1, then 0, then 2). The brute-force (LB) check `_check_lb_brute` correctly returns True. But the fast O(n²) check `_check_lb` returns False, and `_check_lb_debug` (called from `_local_repair`) prints the mismatch to stdout, which the runner treats as invalid output.

## ROOT_CAUSE
The fast check `_check_lb` has an incorrect reduction of the (LB) 4-point property. Its docstring claims:

> For each a, the (b, c) pairs that need a witness are: b in (N(a) \ N(a-1)) and c in (N(a-1) \ N(a))

This is wrong. The actual (LB) condition at position a is: for all b > a, c > b with **ab NOT in E** and **ac in E**, a witness d < a must exist. The correct candidate sets are:

- b candidates: vertices at positions > a that are **NOT** neighbours of va → `~ma & ~prefix & ~(1<<va)`
- c candidates: vertices at positions > b that **ARE** neighbours of va → `ma & ~prefix & ~(1<<va)`

The code instead computes:
```python
na = ma & ~prefix & ~(1 << va)   # N(a): neighbours of va after a
bset = na & ~prev_mask            # WRONG: b must be a NON-neighbour of va
cset = prev_mask & ~na            # WRONG: c must be a neighbour of va
```

`bset` is a subset of `na` (neighbours of va), but the (LB) condition only applies when ab is NOT in E. So the fast check tests the wrong set of (b, c) pairs.

Tracing the failing case (order=[1,0,2], adj[1]=0b1, adj[0]=0b110, adj[2]=0b1):

- a=0, va=1, ma=0b1, prefix=0, prev_mask=0:
  - na = 0b1 & ~0 & ~0b10 = 0b1 (vertex 0 at position 1 is a neighbour of vertex 1)
  - bset = 0b1 & ~0 = 0b1, cset = 0 & ~0b1 = 0. No pairs.
- a=1, va=0, ma=0b110, prefix=0b10, prev_mask=0b1:
  - na = 0b110 & ~0b10 & ~0b01 = 0b100 (vertex 2 at position 2 is a neighbour of vertex 0)
  - bset = 0b100 & ~0b1 = 0b100 (vertex 2 is in N(a) but not in N(a-1))
  - cset = 0b1 & ~0b100 = 0b1 (vertex 0 is in N(a-1) but not in N(a))
  - Check pair (b=2, c=0): witness = adj[2] & ~adj[0] & prefix = 0b1 & ~0b110 & 0b10 = 0b1 & 0b...1001 & 0b10 = 0. Returns False.

But this pair is spurious: b=vertex 2 is a NEIGHBOUR of va=vertex 0 (ab IS in E), so the (LB) condition does not apply. The brute-force check correctly skips this pair because it checks `if (ma >> vb) & 1: continue` (skip if ab in E).

The fast check's reduction is fundamentally flawed: it uses N(a) \ N(a-1) for b-candidates, but the (LB) condition requires b to be a non-neighbour of va, not a neighbour. The correct b-candidates are `~ma & ~prefix & ~(1<<va)` (non-neighbours of va at positions > a), and the correct c-candidates are `ma & ~prefix & ~(1<<va)` (neighbours of va at positions > a, further constrained to be after b).

## REPAIR_HINTS
1. **Fix `_check_lb`**: Replace the bset/cset computation with the correct sets:
   - b candidates: `~ma & ~prefix & ~(1 << va)` (non-neighbours of va at positions > a)
   - c candidates: `ma & ~prefix & ~(1 << va)` (neighbours of va at positions > a)
   - For each b in bset, c in cset with c > b (position constraint), check witness `(adj[b] & ~adj[c] & prefix) != 0`.
   - Note: the position constraint c > b must be enforced. Since bset and cset are vertex bitmasks (not position-ordered), you need to iterate and check that the position of c is greater than the position of b. This may require a position lookup table or a different iteration strategy.

2. **Remove `_check_lb_debug` from the production path**: `_local_repair` calls `_check_lb_debug` which prints to stdout. Even after fixing `_check_lb`, the debug function should not be called in production. Replace the call in `_local_repair` with `_check_lb`.

3. **The `_cheap_insert_ok` function** may also have the same conceptual error (using neighbour/non-neighbour sets incorrectly). After fixing `_check_lb`, verify that `_cheap_insert_ok`'s logic is consistent with the correct (LB) condition. The cheap check is a necessary condition, so it must not reject valid positions. If it uses the same flawed N(a) \ N(a-1) logic, it will incorrectly reject positions and cause unnecessary full recomputes (but not incorrectness, since the final check catches it).

4. **Approaches that will not work**: Simply swapping bset and cset (using `prev_mask & ~na` for b and `na & ~prev_mask` for c) does not fix the issue, because the fundamental problem is that b must be a non-neighbour of va, not a neighbour. The N(a) \ N(a-1) formulation is not a valid reduction of the (LB) condition.
