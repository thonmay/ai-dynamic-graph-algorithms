i.e. skip b if edge (v,b) = (0,1) exists. The (LB) test then computes:

1. **Immediate fix (guarantees correctness):** In `insert_edge`, replace the O(n) `_check_lb_insert` call with the full `_check_lb_full` check. This costs O(n^2) per insertion but eliminates the unsound fast path. The local-repair and recompute fallbacks remain as optimizations for when the full check fails.

2. **Debug the O(n) check:** If the O(n) check is to be kept, verify the role assignment in both branches. In the `pv < pu` branch (a=v, c=u), the (LB) property requires: for b between a and c, if ab ∉ E, then ∃ d < a with db ∈ E and dc ∉ E. The code's guard `if adj[v] & (1 << b): continue` correctly skips b when ab ∈ E. The witness test `W = adj[b] & ~adj[u]` computes {d : db ∈ E and dc ∉ E}, and `W & prefix[pu]` restricts to d < a. This logic appears correct in isolation, but the interaction with the guard may be the issue.

3. **Do NOT rely on `_local_repair` as a safety net:** It only tries 9 specific moves and may not find a valid ordering even when one exists. It is an optimization, not a correctness guarantee.

4. **Do NOT rely on the `_lexbfs` fallback being reached:** The bug is that the check passes, so the fallback is never invoked. Fixing the check is essential.
