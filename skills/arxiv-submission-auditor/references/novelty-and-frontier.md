# Novelty and frontier audit

Use this protocol for claims such as `first`, `new`, `resolves`, `open`, `exact`, `optimal`, `best known`, or a newly established frontier value.

## Novelty ledger

Record:

- the exact theorem/result in normalized mathematical language;
- every materially different model: unrestricted, quadratic, bilinear, monotone, formal algebraic, randomized, uniform/nonuniform, or other relevant restriction;
- search queries, databases, citation chains, survey/reference sources, dates, and scope limits;
- primary evidence that the broader question was open or that earlier work proved only a restricted model;
- nearest prior results and why each does not subsume the manuscript;
- residual uncertainty and the wording permitted by that uncertainty.

Do not infer novelty from the absence of a search result. Do not use a paper's own bibliography, snippets, secondary summaries, or AI recollection as final evidence. Verify the cited open-status statement in the primary source whenever possible.

## Exact-frontier gate

Before presenting a new exact value at size `n` as a frontier:

- prove or cite all smaller cases needed to establish the sequence/frontier;
- state whether each value is in the same computational/algebraic model;
- put the model qualifier before the headline number when the number is classical in another model;
- distinguish an exhaustive certificate or regression program from proof premises;
- ensure the abstract, introduction, theorem statement, metadata, and repository use identical model terminology.

Mark broad `resolves`/`first`/`open` language as a blocker when the search scope, model distinction, or primary evidence is incomplete. Prefer bounded wording such as `to the best of our knowledge, within <documented scope>` only when that accurately reflects the evidence.
